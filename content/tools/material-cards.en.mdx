<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Scope">
This engineering materials database provides mechanical, thermal, and metallurgical properties for structural steels, alloyed engineering steels, stainless steels, aluminum alloys, and engineering thermoplastics conforming to EN, ISO, DIN, and ASTM standards.
</Callout>

## Material Classifications & Properties

- **Structural & Carbon Steels (S235JR, S355J2, C45):** General machine frames, structural weldments, and shafts; characterized by high toughness, predictable yield plateaus, and excellent weldability.
- **Quenched & Tempered Alloy Steels (42CrMo4+QT, 34CrNiMo6):** High torsional and bending fatigue strength; ideal for crankshafts, connecting rods, high-load gears, and drive spindles.
- **Austenitic Stainless Steels (AISI 304 / 1.4301, AISI 316L / 1.4404):** Superior corrosion and chemical resistance in food, marine, and chemical process equipment.
- **Structural Aluminum Alloys (6061-T6, 7075-T6):** Approximately one-third the density of steel ($2.7\text{ g/cm}^3$ vs $7.85\text{ g/cm}^3$); 7075-T6 delivers structural steel yield strengths ($> 480\text{ MPa}$) for aerospace and high-speed robotic linkages.
- **Engineering Thermoplastics (POM-C, PA6, PEEK):** Low friction coefficient, self-lubricating capability, excellent electrical insulation, and high chemical resistance.

## Critical Mechanical Warnings

<Callout type="warning" title="Modulus of Elasticity vs Heat Treatment">
Heat treatment, quenching, and tempering change steel yield and tensile strength, but have **zero effect on Young's modulus ($E = 210\text{ GPa}$)**. If an assembly suffers from excessive elastic deflection or vibration, upgrading from S355 to 42CrMo4 will not reduce deflection; only increasing structural second moment of area ($I$) or beam geometry improves stiffness.
</Callout>

# Examples

## Example 1: High-Speed Robotic Arm Linkage

Selecting material for a high-acceleration pick-and-place end effector linkage where payload mass and inertia govern motor sizing.

**Inputs**
- Maximum operating bending stress: $160\text{ MPa}$
- Cyclic duty: High-speed reversal ($> 10^7$ cycles)
- Primary objective: Minimize moving inertia ($m \propto \rho$) while maintaining safety factor $S_F \ge 2.0$.

**Comparison**
- **S355 Steel:** Density $7.85\text{ g/cm}^3$, Yield $355\text{ MPa}$, Safety factor $S_F = 355 / 160 = 2.22$. Relative weight baseline: $100\%$.
- **6061-T6 Aluminum:** Density $2.70\text{ g/cm}^3$, Yield $275\text{ MPa}$, Safety factor $S_F = 275 / 160 = 1.72$ (Marginal).
- **7075-T6 Aerospace Aluminum:** Density $2.81\text{ g/cm}^3$, Yield $505\text{ MPa}$, Safety factor $S_F = 505 / 160 = 3.16$. Relative weight: $35.8\%$ ($64.2\%$ mass reduction compared to steel).
- **Decision:** Specify 7075-T6 for drastic reduction in motor driving torque and rapid cycle times with ample structural safety.

## Example 2: Industrial Power Transmission Shaft

Selecting material for a high-torque gearbox output shaft subjected to alternating torsional fatigue.

**Inputs**
- Calculated equivalent dynamic shear stress: $\tau_{eq} = 220\text{ MPa}$
- Keyway stress concentration factor: $K_t = 2.4$

**Comparison**
- **C45 Normalized:** Yield strength $370\text{ MPa}$, Torsional yield $\tau_y \approx 0.58 \times 370 = 214\text{ MPa}$. Overstressed under notch factor.
- **42CrMo4+QT (Quenched & Tempered):** Yield strength $750\text{ MPa}$, Torsional yield $\tau_y \approx 0.58 \times 750 = 435\text{ MPa}$. Safety factor $S_F = 435 / 220 = 1.98$. Excellent deep hardenability and fatigue limit ($S_e \approx 450\text{ MPa}$).
- **Decision:** Specify 42CrMo4+QT with radiused keyway transitions and ground bearing journals.
