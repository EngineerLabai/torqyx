## Governing Formulas & Mechanical Properties

Tensile stress area (As) calculation per ISO 898-1 and nominal yield/proof capacity:

<Formula label="Tensile Stress Area and Yield Load (ISO 898-1)">
As = (π / 4) · (d - 0.9382 · P)² | F_yield = As · Re | F_proof = As · Sp
</Formula>

Under ISO 898-1 property class designation X.Y:
- Nominal tensile strength: Rm = X × 100 MPa
- Nominal yield strength: Re = X × Y × 10 MPa (or 0.2% proof strength Rp0.2)
- Proof load stress: Sp ≈ 0.90 · Re (typical proof stress ratio)

## Fastener Assembly Warnings

<Callout type="warning" title="Nut & Bolt Class Compatibility">
Always match bolt strength classes with corresponding nut proof load classes conforming to ISO 898-2 (e.g., use Class 8 nuts with Grade 8.8 bolts, Class 10 nuts with Grade 10.9 bolts). Pairing high-strength bolts with undersized nuts causes catastrophic thread stripping before the bolt reaches design clamping preload.
</Callout>

# Examples

## Example 1: M16 Grade 8.8 vs Grade 10.9 Load Capacity

Comparing the tensile capacity of M16 standard coarse fasteners (P = 2.0 mm).

**Inputs**
- Nominal diameter: d = 16 mm
- Thread pitch: P = 2.0 mm (coarse)
- Tensile stress area: As = (π/4) × (16 - 0.9382 × 2.0)² = 156.67 mm² ≈ 157 mm²

**Outputs**
- Grade 8.8 (Rm = 800 MPa, Re = 640 MPa):
  - Yield load: F_yield = 157 × 640 = 100.48 kN
  - Proof load (Sp = 580 MPa): F_proof = 157 × 580 = 91.06 kN
- Grade 10.9 (Rm = 1000 MPa, Re = 900 MPa):
  - Yield load: F_yield = 157 × 900 = 141.30 kN
  - Proof load (Sp = 830 MPa): F_proof = 157 × 830 = 130.31 kN
- Upgrading from Grade 8.8 to 10.9 yields a 40.6% increase in clamp load within the exact same envelope diameter.

## Example 2: Metric Coarse vs Fine Thread Tensile Advantage

Evaluating cross-sectional advantage of M12 fine thread (P = 1.25 mm) over coarse thread (P = 1.75 mm).

**Inputs**
- Fastener: M12 Grade 10.9 (Re = 900 MPa)

**Outputs**
- Coarse pitch (P = 1.75 mm): As = (π/4) × (12 - 0.9382 × 1.75)² = 84.27 mm²
  - Yield capacity: F_yield = 84.27 × 900 = 75.84 kN
- Fine pitch (P = 1.25 mm): As = (π/4) × (12 - 0.9382 × 1.25)² = 92.07 mm²
  - Yield capacity: F_yield = 92.07 × 900 = 82.86 kN
- Fine pitch provides 9.3% higher tensile stress area and superior resistance against vibrational self-loosening.
