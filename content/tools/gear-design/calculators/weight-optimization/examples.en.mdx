## Example 1: Webbed Steel Bull Gear Weight Reduction

Calculating mass and volume for an industrial steel spur gear.

**Inputs**
- Outer diameter: $D = 250\text{ mm}$
- Bore diameter: $d = 60\text{ mm}$
- Face width: $b = 40\text{ mm}$
- Material density: $\rho = 7850\text{ kg/m}^3$ (alloy steel)
- Lightening factor: $0.20$ ($20\%$ mass reduction via web pockets)

**Outputs**
- Solid annular volume: $V = (\pi / 4) \times (250^2 - 60^2) \times 40 = 1.850 \times 10^6\text{ mm}^3 = 0.00185\text{ m}^3$
- Solid blank mass: $m_{solid} = 0.00185 \times 7850 = 14.53\text{ kg}$
- Lightened mass: $m_{light} = 14.53 \times (1 - 0.20) = 11.62\text{ kg}$
- Net mass savings: $\Delta m = 2.91\text{ kg}$

## Example 2: High-Speed Aluminum Pinion

Evaluating rotational inertia and mass for an aerospace actuator gear.

**Inputs**
- Outer diameter: $D = 80\text{ mm}$
- Shaft bore: $d = 20\text{ mm}$
- Face width: $b = 18\text{ mm}$
- Material density: $\rho = 2810\text{ kg/m}^3$ (7075-T6 aluminum)
- Lightening factor: $0.10$

**Outputs**
- Solid volume: $V = 8.482 \times 10^4\text{ mm}^3 = 8.482 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3$
- Solid mass: $m_{solid} = 8.482 \times 10^{-5} \times 2810 = 0.238\text{ kg}$
- Lightened mass: $m_{light} = 0.238 \times 0.90 = 0.214\text{ kg}$
- Low rotor mass significantly suppresses motor starting torque spikes and dynamic torsional vibration.
