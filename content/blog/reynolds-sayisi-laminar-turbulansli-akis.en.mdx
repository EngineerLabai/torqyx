## What Does the Reynolds Number Quantify?

The Reynolds number (`Re`) is a dimensionless ratio comparing inertial forces to viscous forces within a moving fluid:


`Re = (ρ  *  v  *  D / μ) = (v  *  D / ν)`


Where:
- `ρ`: Fluid density [`kg/m ^3`]
- `v`: Mean flow velocity [`m/s`]
- `D`: Pipe inner diameter (characteristic length) [`m`]
- `μ`: Dynamic viscosity [`Pa  *  s` or `kg/(m  *  s)`]
- `ν = μ / ρ`: Kinematic viscosity [`m ^2/ s`]

Because it encapsulates the fluid's propensity to either slide smoothly in layers or break up into chaotic eddies, `Re` serves as the primary diagnostic tool in hydraulic and aerodynamic engineering.

## Laminar Flow Regime (`Re &lt; 2300`)

In laminar flow, fluid particles travel in parallel streamlines without macroscopic cross-mixing:
- Viscous damping dominates inertial momentum.
- The velocity profile across the pipe diameter is parabolic, with centerline velocity reaching twice the average velocity (`v_max = 2 v_avg`).
- The Darcy friction factor is independent of pipe wall roughness and follows Hagen-Poiseuille law: `f = 64 / Re`.
- Prevalent in heavy lubricants, hydraulic oils, microfluidics, and low-velocity viscous liquids.

## Turbulent Flow Regime (`Re > 4000`)

In turbulent flow, inertial momentum overwhelms viscous shear, triggering chaotic velocity fluctuations, secondary recirculation, and vortex shedding:
- The velocity profile flattens across the core, producing steep velocity gradients near the pipe wall.
- Fluid mixing and convective heat transfer rates increase substantially, but at the expense of steep hydraulic friction and pressure drop.
- The friction factor depends on both `Re` and relative surface roughness (`epsilon / D`), governed by the implicit Colebrook-White equation.

## The Critical Transition Zone (`2300  <=  Re  <=  4000`)

The boundary between 2,300 and 4,000 is not a rigid barrier. Pipe vibrations, abrupt pump starts, fitting disturbances, and wall roughness can trigger premature turbulence. In hydraulic design, systems operating in the transitional zone exhibit unpredictable pressure fluctuations and noise; pipes are typically sized to keep flow stably laminar or firmly turbulent.

## Temperature and Viscosity Sensitivity

Fluid viscosity is intensely temperature-dependent. For ISO VG 46 mineral hydraulic oil:
- At 20 °C (`ν  ≈  120  cSt`), flow through a return line may be completely laminar (`Re &lt; 1000`).
- At 60 °C (`ν  ≈  25  cSt`), the same volumetric flow accelerates `Re` fivefold, transitioning the line into turbulent flow and fundamentally altering cooling and pressure dynamics.
