## The Core Distinction: Unthreaded Shank vs Full-Length Thread

DIN 931 and DIN 933 are the most prevalent metric hexagon head bolt specifications in global manufacturing. The fundamental geometric distinction is straightforward:
- **DIN 931 (ISO 4014):** Features an unthreaded smooth shank extending from under the head, with threading applied only over a standardized portion of the nominal length.
- **DIN 933 (ISO 4017):** Threaded continuously right up to the underside of the hex head (full thread).

While this appears simple on paper, selecting between full-thread and partial-thread bolts significantly alters joint shear capacity, stress concentrations, alignment accuracy, and cyclic fatigue behavior.

## When to Specify DIN 931 (Partial Thread)

A partially threaded bolt provides substantial advantages in structural and precision shear joints:
1. **Shear Plane on Solid Shank:** The unthreaded body provides a larger cross-sectional area ($A = \pi d^2 / 4$) compared to the reduced thread stress area ($A_s \approx 0.78 \times A$). Bearing against a smooth shank avoids notch stress concentrations in the shear plane.
2. **Precision Alignment:** Solid shanks prevent bolt hole play and minimize joint slip in dowelled or fitted assemblies.
3. **Fatigue Endurance:** Thread runout situated away from the interface plane increases dynamic fatigue life under fluctuating transverse loads.

*Design Constraint:* The unthreaded shank length ($l - b$) must match the total clamped grip thickness. If the shank is too long, the nut bottom out against the thread runout before clamping preload is achieved.

## When to Specify DIN 933 (Full Thread)

Fully threaded bolts are the standard choice for versatile assembly and general construction:
1. **Grip Thickness Flexibility:** Ideal for maintenance, variable plate stacks, and clamping varying thickness spacers without stocking multiple bolt lengths.
2. **Tapped Blind Holes:** Essential when threading into tapped holes where maximum thread engagement is required close to the joint surface.
3. **Tension-Dominated Joints:** In pure axial tension (e.g., pipe flanges, pressure vessel covers), thread location relative to the interface is less critical because shear stresses are negligible.

## ISO Equivalents and Drawing Callouts

In modern European and international engineering standards:
- **DIN 931** corresponds closely to **ISO 4014**.
- **DIN 933** corresponds closely to **ISO 4017**.

*Procurement Best Practice:* An engineering bill of materials (BOM) should never call out "M12 bolt" in isolation. Always specify diameter, pitch, length, standard, property class, and plating:  
`Hex Bolt M12 x 1.75 x 80, Grade 8.8, ISO 4014 (DIN 931), Zinc Flake ISO 10683`.
