Bolt preload is one of the most critical starting parameters for ensuring a bolted joint operates safely without loosening or fatigue failure. The VDI 2230 guideline treats this value not merely as a generic torque lookup table, but as a coupled system involving bolt elasticity, clamped member resilience, working loads, and tightening scatter.

## What is Bolt Preload?

- The clamping force exerted by the fastener onto the joint interface after assembly tightening.
- Directly governed by thread geometry, lead angle, material grade, and friction coefficients at both thread and under-head surfaces.
- Insufficient preload causes joint separation, fatigue cycling, or self-loosening under vibration. Excessive preload risks permanent thread yield, shear stripping, or flange crushing.

## The VDI 2230 Systematic Method

- Tightening preload, embedment relaxation, dynamic operating forces, and safety factors are evaluated simultaneously.
- Elastic resilience of both the bolt shank and the clamped joint components are factored into the force-displacement diagram (joint diagram).
- Standard assumptions must always be cross-referenced with actual operating boundary conditions and thermal expansions.

## Step-by-Step Calculation Flow

- Identify nominal bolt diameter, thread pitch, property class (e.g., 8.8, 10.9, 12.9), and coefficient of friction range.
- Calculate the stress cross-sectional area and allowable proof load margin.
- Derive the target clamp load and the corresponding nominal tightening torque with the tightening factor.

## Practical Engineering Verification

The most common failure in preload calculation is assuming friction is an exact constant. An identical M10 or M12 fastener produces drastically different clamp loads on dry, lightly oiled, zinc-plated, or PTFE-lubricated surfaces. Therefore, before transferring calculated torque to the factory floor, washer condition, thread lubrication, torque wrench calibration, and tightening method scatter must be validated.

In a professional mechanical engineering workflow, once the target preload is calculated, three essential checks must be confirmed: Does the fastener stress remain within allowable yield limits, do clamped members maintain compression across all dynamic loading phases, and is the joint resilient against worst-case assembly scatter?
