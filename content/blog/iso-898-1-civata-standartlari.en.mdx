ISO 898-1 is the primary international engineering standard defining the mechanical and physical properties of metric bolts, screws, and studs manufactured from carbon steel and alloy steel. In mechanical design and plant engineering, the raised numeric markings found on fastener heads—such as "8.8", "10.9", or "12.9"—strictly correspond to this standardized classification system.

## The mathematical logic of ISO 898-1 property class codes

The two-digit designation separated by a dot ($X.Y$) mathematically specifies both ultimate tensile strength and proof yield strength:
1. **First Digit ($X$):** Represents one-hundredth of the nominal minimum ultimate tensile strength ($R_m$):
   $$R_m = X \times 100 \quad [\text{MPa}]$$
2. **Second Digit ($Y$):** Multiplied by 10, expresses the ratio of lower yield strength ($R_{eL}$) or 0.2% proof stress ($R_{p0.2}$) relative to the nominal tensile strength:
   $$R_{p0.2} = R_m \times \frac{Y}{10} = (X \times 100) \times \frac{Y}{10} = X \times Y \times 10 \quad [\text{MPa}]$$

### Core property class mechanical specifications

| Property Class | Min. Tensile Strength $R_m$ [$\text{MPa}$] | Min. Yield Strength $R_{p0.2}$ [$\text{MPa}$] | Yield-to-Tensile Ratio | Core Hardness (HRC) | Elongation at Fracture ($A_5$) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **4.6** | $400\text{ MPa}$ | $240\text{ MPa}$ | 60% | $67 - 95\text{ HRB}$ | $\ge 22\%$ |
| **5.8** | $500\text{ MPa}$ | $400\text{ MPa}$ | 80% | $82 - 95\text{ HRB}$ | $\ge 10\%$ |
| **8.8** | $800\text{ MPa}$ | $640\text{ MPa}$ | 80% | $22 - 32\text{ HRC}$ | $\ge 12\%$ |
| **10.9** | $1000\text{ MPa}$ | $900\text{ MPa}$ | 90% | $32 - 39\text{ HRC}$ | $\ge 9\%$ |
| **12.9** | $1200\text{ MPa}$ | $1080\text{ MPa}$ | 90% | $39 - 44\text{ HRC}$ | $\ge 8\%$ |

## Selecting between Class 8.8, 10.9, and 12.9

- **Class 8.8 (Structural & Machine Engineering):** Produced from medium carbon quenched and tempered steel. Balances high tensile capability with 12% elongation and superior notch impact toughness, making it the standard baseline for heavy machinery, gearboxes, and structural steel connections.
- **Class 10.9 (Automotive & High-Torque Machinery):** Manufactured from quenched and tempered alloy steels (e.g., 42CrMo4). Preferred in automotive chassis, engine cylinder heads, and wind turbine slewing bearings where high clamp force is needed within compact geometries.
- **Class 12.9 (Ultra-High Strength):** Delivers the highest tensile capacity ($1200\text{ MPa}$), but exhibits reduced ductility ($8\%$ elongation). Fasteners above $39\text{ HRC}$ are susceptible to hydrogen embrittlement during acid pickling or electroplating, mandating strict de-embrittlement baking.

## Nut compatibility rule (ISO 898-2)

In any engineered bolted joint, the failure mode under overload must be bolt shank tensile fracture rather than internal thread stripping. Stripping is gradual, difficult to detect during visual inspection, and structurally catastrophic. Per ISO 898-2, the mating nut property class must match or exceed the bolt rating:
- Pair Class 8.8 bolts with **Class 8** nuts,
- Pair Class 10.9 bolts with **Class 10** nuts,
- Pair Class 12.9 bolts with **Class 12** nuts.

## Industrial worked example

For an M16 Class 8.8 bolt with effective tensile stress area $A_s = 157\text{ mm}^2$:
- Yield force:
  $$F_y = R_{p0.2} \cdot A_s = 640\text{ MPa} \times 157\text{ mm}^2 = \mathbf{100\,480\text{ N}} \approx \mathbf{100.5\text{ kN}}$$
- Maximum allowable assembly tightening preload per VDI 2230 (90% yield criterion):
  $$F_{M,max} = 0.90 \cdot F_y = 0.90 \times 100.5\text{ kN} = \mathbf{90.4\text{ kN}}$$
