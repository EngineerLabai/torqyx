## L10 is Not an Individual Failure Timestamp

In mechanical engineering, the L_10 bearing life represents a standardized statistical metric: it is the basic rating life associated with 90% reliability. This means 90% of a sufficiently large population of identical bearings operating under identical conditions will reach or exceed this duration before the initial onset of subsurface material fatigue (spalling or flaking).

L_10 is conventionally expressed in millions of revolutions. To convert this into operating hours (L_10h), rotational speed (n in rpm) must be accounted for.

## The Governing ISO 281 Formula

The foundational ISO 281 formula connects dynamic load capacity (C) and equivalent dynamic bearing load (P):


`L_10 = ( (C / P) )^p   [ million revolutions ]`


Where the life exponent p is:
- **p = 3** for ball bearings (point contact)
- **p = 10/3  ≈  3.333** for roller bearings (line contact)

Because C/P is raised to the 3rd or 3.33rd power, small increases in load produce drastic reductions in fatigue life: doubling the load cuts ball bearing life by a factor of 8 (2^3 = 8).

## Converting to Operating Hours (L_10h)

To determine service life in running hours:


`L_10h = (10^6 / 60  *  n)  *  ( (C / P) )^p   [ hours ]`


For example, a bearing carrying a given load at 1,500 rpm will accumulate revolutions five times faster than at 300 rpm, reducing L_10h by 80% even though total million-revolution fatigue life remains constant.

## Why Field Life Diverges from Catalog Calculations

ISO 281 basic rating life assumes ideal elastohydrodynamic lubrication (EHL), pristine lubricant cleanliness, zero shaft misalignment, and steady loads. In actual industrial machinery:
1. **Lubricant Viscosity and Film Thickness (kappa):** Operating below the minimum required kinematic viscosity leads to metal-to-metal boundary contact and micro-spalling.
2. **Solid Contamination:** Ingress of abrasive particles dents raceways, creating stress risers that trigger premature fatigue.
3. **Shaft Deflection and Angular Misalignment:** Tilting exceeding 2 to 10 arcminutes concentrates contact stress at roller edges.

For mission-critical gearboxes and turbomachinery, the extended ISO 281 life rating (L_nm = a_1  *  a_ISO  *  L_10) must be utilized.

## Common Calculation Mistakes

- **Neglecting Axial Thrust from Helical Gearing:** Evaluating radial reaction force alone while ignoring thrust load yields a dangerously understated equivalent load P.
- **Unit Mismatches:** Entering capacity C in kN and equivalent load P in N causes an error of 10^9 in calculated life.
- **Using the Ball Exponent for Roller Bearings:** Applying p = 3 instead of p = 10/3 introduces systematic sizing errors.
