## Two Fundamentally Distinct Tolerance Concepts

In precision machine design, the phrase "bearing tolerance" frequently causes confusion between two distinct technical concepts:

1. **Bearing Manufacturing Tolerances (ISO 492):** The dimensional accuracy of the rolling bearing itself, including bore diameter (d), outer diameter (D), ring width (B), and radial runout. Governed by precision classes Normal (P0), P6, P5, and P4.
2. **Shaft and Housing Fits (ISO 286):** The machining tolerance fields specified on manufacturing drawings for the mating shaft diameter and the housing bore (e.g., k5, m5, H7, J7).

A 6205 bearing is manufactured to tight ISO 492 limits; however, how it performs inside your machine depends entirely on the ISO 286 fit classes you specify for the shaft seat and housing bore.

## How to Read the ISO 286 Fit System

Under the ISO 286 limit system:
- **Letters denote tolerance zone position** relative to the nominal dimension:
  - **Capital Letters (e.g., H, J, N):** Designate internal features (housing bores).
  - **Lowercase Letters (e.g., h, k, m, p):** Designate external features (shaft diameters).
- **Numbers denote the International Tolerance (IT) Grade** (e.g., IT5, IT6, IT7), defining the width of the tolerance band. Smaller numbers signify tighter tolerances.

For instance, an H7 housing bore provides a unilateral clearance zone starting at zero above nominal. A k5 or m5 shaft seat establishes a controlled interference fit designed to clamp the bearing inner ring firmly.

## The Cardinal Rule: Rotating Ring vs. Stationary Ring Loading

Choosing between a loose clearance fit and a tight interference fit is governed strictly by the **nature of the applied load relative to ring rotation**:

### 1. Rotating Inner Ring with Stationary Direction Load (Most Common)
- **Kinematics:** The shaft and inner ring rotate together while the radial load vector points in a fixed direction (e.g., gravity or belt pull).
- **Physical Effect:** Every point on the inner ring raceway passes through the load zone during every revolution (**circumferential load**). If fitted loosely, the inner ring will slip and creep around the shaft, causing aggressive fretting corrosion, shaft grooving, and severe heat.
- **Rule:** The inner ring **must have an interference fit** (typically j6, k5, or m5).
- **Housing Ring:** The outer ring remains stationary relative to the load (**point load**). It should be fitted with a **light clearance or transition fit** (typically H7 or J7), enabling smooth axial floating to accommodate thermal expansion.

### 2. Rotating Outer Ring / Unbalance Load
- When the outer ring rotates relative to the load direction (e.g., conveyor idler pulleys, vibrating screens, wheel hubs), the rule reverses: the **outer ring requires an interference fit** (P7 or N7) while the inner ring has a loose fit (g6 or h6).

## Impact on Bearing Internal Clearance

When a bearing inner ring is pressed onto an interference-fit shaft, the inner ring expands radially. Conversely, pressing an outer ring into a tight housing contracts the outer raceway.

This geometric expansion reduces the bearing's **radial internal clearance** by approximately 80% of the effective interference:


`Δ e  ≈  0.8  *  Δ d_ interference`


If an engineer specifies an interference fit on a bearing with "Normal" (CN) clearance, the residual clearance can drop below zero under operating temperature, causing preload pinching, elevated operating temperatures (>100^circ C), rapid lubricant oxidation, and premature bearing seizure. In such assemblies, specifying **C3 clearance** (greater than normal internal clearance) is vital.

## Differential Thermal Expansion: Steel vs. Light Alloy Housings

In electric motors or gearboxes utilizing aluminum or cast magnesium housings:
- Aluminum expands at roughly twice the rate of bearing chrome steel (α_ Al  ≈  23  *  10^-6/ K vs. α_ Steel  ≈  11  *  10^-6/ K).
- A housing bore fit that provides a snug J7 transition fit at 20^circ C can expand into a loose clearance fit at an operating temperature of 80^circ C, allowing the outer ring to spin in its seat.
- Engineers must calculate thermal fit growth across the full operational temperature envelope or insert cast iron bushing sleeves.

## Common Shaft & Housing Fit Combinations

| Application Condition | Shaft Diameter Fit (ISO 286) | Housing Bore Fit (ISO 286) | Typical Clearance Class |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Electric Motor (Normal Load)** | k5 (up to varnothing 50), m5 (> varnothing 50) | H7 (floating side), J7 (locating) | Normal (CN) or C3 |
| **High Radial / Heavy Shock Duty** | m5 / n6 | K7 / M7 | C3 |
| **High-Speed Spindle (Precision)** | h5 / j5 | H6 / J6 | Special Preload |
| **Conveyor Idler Rollers (Outer Ring Rotates)** | h6 / g6 (loose) | M7 / P7 (tight) | Normal |
