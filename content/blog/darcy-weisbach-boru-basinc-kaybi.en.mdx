In industrial piping systems, pressure loss must be verified during early mechanical design to ensure proper pump head sizing and operating efficiency. The Darcy-Weisbach equation unifies pipe inner diameter, length, flow velocity, fluid density, and friction factor into a rigorous, verifiable calculation model.

## Governing Hydraulic Theory

The Darcy-Weisbach head loss ($h_f$ in meters) and pressure drop ($\Delta p$ in Pascals) are expressed as:

$$\Delta p = f \cdot \frac{L}{D} \cdot \frac{\rho \cdot v^2}{2}$$

$$h_f = f \cdot \frac{L}{D} \cdot \frac{v^2}{2g}$$

- **Pipe Geometry:** Diameter ($D$) and length ($L$) dictate boundary shear area.
- **Fluid Velocity:** Flow velocity ($v = 4Q / (\pi D^2)$) impacts pressure loss quadratically ($v^2$).
- **Friction Factor ($f$):** Evaluated from flow regime and surface roughness via the Colebrook-White equation.

## Determining the Friction Factor

- In laminar flow ($Re < 2300$): $f = 64 / Re$.
- In turbulent flow ($Re > 4000$): The friction factor is determined iteratively or via Swamee-Jain approximation accounting for relative pipe roughness ($\epsilon / D$).

## Engineering Interpretation

Piping pressure drop does more than calculate kPa loss at the outlet; it directly dictates annual electrical pumping energy. Sizing pipes too small reduces initial piping CAPEX but skyrockets lifetime operational pump power. Sizing pipes generously cuts energy consumption and mitigates erosion and water hammer risks.
