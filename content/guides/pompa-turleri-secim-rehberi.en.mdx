<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Usage Note">
This guide details the sizing and technology selection of industrial process pumps based on head requirements ($H$), net positive suction head ($NPSH$), viscosity derating, and fluid rheology in accordance with ISO 5199, ISO 9906, and API 610. For abrasive slurries or non-Newtonian polymer melts, specialized sealing and materials are required.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

Selecting the wrong pump architecture degrades process efficiency and inflates operating and maintenance expenditures. Sizing driven purely by flow rate catalog ratings introduces severe liabilities:
- Centrifugal pumps suffer catastrophic head and flow derating when handling viscous oils or slurries,
- Inadequate net positive suction head generates vapor bubble implosions (cavitation), pitting impellers and causing severe vibration,
- Positive displacement pumps operating against closed discharge valves cause instant overpressurization, line rupture, or motor burnout.

## Assumptions

- The fluid behaves as a single-phase Newtonian liquid (Hydraulic Institute viscosity correction factors apply for viscous oils).
- System head losses, piping elevation changes, and friction head are established across minimum and maximum flow envelopes.
- The operating point resides between 80% and 110% of the pump Best Efficiency Point (BEP).

## Step by step method

1. **Select Pump Architecture Based on Fluid Viscosity:**
   - **Centrifugal Pumps (Radial / Mixed / Axial Flow):** Preferred for low-viscosity fluids ($\mu < 150\text{ cSt}$ / water, chemicals, light hydrocarbons), high flow capacity ($Q$), and moderate discharge heads.
   - **Positive Displacement Pumps (Gear, Lobe, Progressive Cavity, Piston):** Mandatory for high-viscosity fluids ($\mu > 150\text{ cSt}$ / heavy oils, resins, slurries), metering duty, or high pressure ($P > 25\text{ bar}$) where flow must remain constant irrespective of discharge backpressure.

2. **Compute Total Dynamic Head ($H$):**
   Sum static elevation difference ($\Delta z$), differential pressure ($\Delta P / \rho g$), and total piping friction losses ($\sum h_f$):
   $$H = \Delta z + \frac{P_{discharge} - P_{suction}}{\rho \cdot g} + \sum h_f \quad [\text{m}]$$

3. **Determine Hydraulic and Motor Shaft Power:**
   Given volumetric flow $Q$ [$\text{m}^3\text{/s}$], dynamic head $H$ [$\text{m}$], and liquid density $\rho$ [$\text{kg/m}^3$]:
   $$P_h = \frac{\rho \cdot g \cdot Q \cdot H}{1000} \quad [\text{kW}]$$
   Accounting for pump hydraulic efficiency ($\eta_p$) and motor safety margin ($S_m \approx 15\% - 20\%$):
   $$P_m = \frac{P_h}{\eta_p} \cdot (1 + S_m)$$

4. **Verify Available Net Positive Suction Head ($NPSH_A$):**
   Evaluate absolute suction head above vapor pressure at the impeller inlet:
   $$NPSH_A = \frac{P_{ambient} - P_{vapor}}{\rho \cdot g} \pm h_{static} - h_{f,suction}$$
   To guarantee cavitation-free service, maintain a positive safety margin over vendor required $NPSH_R$:
   $$NPSH_A \ge NPSH_R + 0.5\text{ m}$$

5. **Industrial Worked Example:**
   A cooling system requires $Q = 36\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$ ($0.01\text{ m}^3\text{/s}$) of clean water ($\rho = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) against a total dynamic head of $H = 45\text{ m}$. The selected radial centrifugal pump offers BEP efficiency $\eta_p = 0.72$ and $NPSH_R = 2.4\text{ m}$:
   - Hydraulic power:
     $$P_h = \frac{1000 \times 9.81 \times 0.01 \times 45}{1000} = \mathbf{4.41\text{ kW}}$$
   - Shaft input power:
     $$P_{shaft} = \frac{4.41}{0.72} = \mathbf{6.13\text{ kW}}$$
   - Standard motor selection with 20% margin:
     $$P_m = 6.13 \times 1.20 = 7.36\text{ kW} \implies \mathbf{7.5\text{ kW}}\text{ standard electric motor}$$
   - Suction from an open atmospheric sump ($1.013\text{ bar}$) with water at 20 °C ($P_v = 0.0234\text{ bar}$), positive head $h_s = +2.0\text{ m}$, suction line loss $h_{f,suction} = 0.6\text{ m}$:
     $$NPSH_A = \frac{101\,300 - 2340}{1000 \times 9.81} + 2.0 - 0.6 = 10.09 + 1.4 = \mathbf{11.49\text{ m}}$$
   - $NPSH_A (11.49\text{ m}) \gg NPSH_R (2.4\text{ m}) + 0.5\text{ m}$, cavitation margin is fully satisfied.

## Common mistakes

- **Omitting Pressure Relief Valves on Positive Displacement Pumps:** While centrifugal pumps can briefly run against a closed valve, PD pumps generate destructive pressures that rupture pipework or burn motors; integral PRVs are essential.
- **Operating Far to the Left of the BEP:** Running below 60% of design flow triggers internal recirculation, thermal cavitation, and destructive radial bearing thrust.
- **Neglecting Vapor Pressure Rise with Temperature:** At 85 °C, water vapor pressure spikes to $0.58\text{ bar}$; failure to provide pressurized suction leads to immediate vapor flashing.
- **Matching Suction Pipe Diameter to Pump Suction Nozzle:** Suction piping must be sized one nominal diameter larger using an eccentric reducer (flat on top) to suppress air pocketing.
