## Problem / Objective

Bearing selection is frequently underestimated in engineering practice. The "larger bearing equals more safety" perception leads to oversizing, unnecessary cost, and sometimes shorter life (internal clearance increases and unloaded-zone fatigue in oversized bearings). Conversely, selecting a low catalog L10 life results in early failure. This guide provides a systematic bearing selection process that integrates load analysis, speed check, lubrication, and life calculation.

## Assumptions

- Radial and axial load components are defined (static and dynamic).
- Rotational speed (rpm) or linear speed is either constant or varies within a known duty cycle spectrum.
- Operating temperature range is known.
- Lubrication method (grease or oil) and relubrication interval are defined.
- Expected service life or target reliability level is stated.

## Step by step method

### Step 1 — Determine load type and magnitude

The starting point of bearing selection is fully defining the force at the shaft-to-bearing interface.

**Equivalent dynamic load P:**

Combined formula for radial and axial load:

`P = X * Fr + Y * Fa`

Where:
- Fr = radial force (N)
- Fa = axial force (N)
- X, Y = catalog coefficients per bearing type (fixed or bidirectional load)

For single-row ball bearings, the axial factor engages when `Fa/Fr exceeds e`. The value e is read from the catalog based on internal geometry.

**Static safety factor S0:**

When impact or vibration load is present: `S0 = C0 / P0` (C0: static load capacity, P0: static equivalent load). Per ISO 76, S0 must be at least 1.5–2.

### Step 2 — Select bearing type

| Bearing Type | Radial Load | Axial Load | Speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deep groove ball | Good | Medium | High | Universal, most common |
| Cylindrical roller (CRB) | Very Good | Poor | High | Accommodates shaft elongation |
| Angular contact ball | Good | Good | Medium-High | Bidirectional axial |
| Tapered roller | Good | Very Good | Medium | Requires preload |
| Spherical roller | Excellent | Medium | Medium | High misalignment tolerance |

Typical selection guidance:
- Pure radial load, high speed → Deep groove ball
- Heavy radial + light axial → Cylindrical roller
- Axial-dominant + high speed → Angular contact (matched pair)
- Heavy combined load, low speed → Tapered roller

### Step 3 — Calculate L10 bearing life (ISO 281)

Basic L10 life (in millions of revolutions):

`L10 = (C / P)^p`

- C = dynamic load rating (N) — catalog value
- P = equivalent dynamic load (N)
- p = 3 (ball bearings), 10/3 (roller bearings)

Converting millions of revolutions to hours:

`Lh = (L10 * 10^6) / (60 * n)`

n = rotational speed (rpm)

**Example:** C = 35 kN, P = 10 kN, n = 1,500 rpm, ball bearing:

- L10 = (35/10)³ = 42.875 million revolutions
- Lh = (42.875 × 10⁶) / (60 × 1,500) = 476 hours

Note: This basic calculation excludes lubrication, temperature, and contamination factors. Modified L10m calculation requires ISO 281 Annex A.

### Step 4 — Check speed limit and lubrication viscosity

Every bearing has two speed limits:
- **Thermal speed limit (catalog):** Maximum operating speed under standard lubrication conditions
- **Kinematic speed limit:** The limit where oil drag force exceeds acceleration forces

Lubrication viscosity: The ISO VG grade must meet the required minimum kinematic viscosity `ν1` at operating temperature. The viscosity ratio `κ = ν / ν1` from the SKF catalog; `κ ≥ 1` indicates optimal film thickness.

For grease: base oil viscosity, thickener type, and operating temperature are evaluated together. Standard lithium complex grease is suitable from –20°C to +120°C.

### Step 5 — Verify tolerance and clearance compatibility

Shaft and housing bore tolerances affect bearing internal clearance. An excessively tight fit can zero out internal clearance, causing temperature rise and early fatigue; too loose a fit leads to vibration and rocking.

Typical recommendations (ISO 286-1):
- Rotating inner ring (rotating load on inner): k5, m5, n6
- Stationary inner ring (point load): h5, js5, k5
- Rotating outer ring: K7, M7
- Stationary outer ring (point load): H7, G7

## Common mistakes

**1. Confusing radial and axial loads**

Failing to calculate axial forces (motor side-pull, gear axial component) at locations where only radial load is expected can reduce bearing life by a factor of 3–5.

**2. Assuming service factor of 1**

A service factor `fs` must be applied for vibration, impact, and misalignment. Well-aligned, vibration-free systems: fs = 1–1.2; moderate vibration: 1.5–2; high impact: 2.5–3.

**3. Not adjusting lubrication viscosity for operating temperature**

Grease and oil viscosity is referenced at 40°C; but bearing operating temperature is different. The actual viscosity needed at 80°C operating temperature can differ substantially from the ISO 40°C classification.

**4. Not checking internal clearance after interference fit**

Interference fit expands the inner ring and reduces internal clearance. Instead of CN (Normal) clearance, C3 or C4 clearance class should be selected to achieve optimal clearance after mounting.

**5. Ignoring thermal shaft elongation**

On long shafts, thermal elongation generates axial load toward the fixed bearing. The free (floating) side bearing must be allowed axial movement (shaft + bore tolerance combination provides this).

## Case summary — TORQYX Engineering Board, Machine Elements Desk

A process pump rotor shaft used a double-row angular contact bearing (C = 42 kN) running at 2,900 rpm selected for its axial load capacity. Failure records showed two bearing replacements within an 8-month period.

Investigation: the Fa/Fr ratio in the P calculation was entered incorrectly; combined load factor Y was overlooked and actual equivalent load P came out 40% higher. Corrected L10h = 2,800 hours (versus the erroneous prior calculation of 6,500 hours). Additionally, the grease relubrication interval was 6,000 hours; correct interval per actual requirements was 2,000 hours. Solution: bearing upgraded to C = 62 kN, relubrication interval revised. Zero failures in the following 24 months.

## Frequently asked questions

### Question: What does L10 life mean?

Per ISO 281, it is the life estimate at which 90% of a group of nominally identical bearings running under identical conditions will survive. L10 = 500,000 revolutions means the threshold corresponding to a 10% failure probability.

### Question: Is a larger bearing always longer-lived?

No. L10 life depends on `(C/P)^3`. A higher C value increases life when P is constant; however, an excessively large bearing introduces increased internal clearance and fatigue stress concentration. Optimum sizing is critical.

### Question: Should I choose grease or oil lubrication?

Grease: low-to-medium speed, easy maintenance, sealing advantage. Oil: high speed, remote feed, oil jacket cooling. The Dn number (bore diameter × rpm) defines the grease limit: Dn greater than 300,000 favors oil.

### Question: If the bearing fit feels too tight during assembly, what should I do?

Measure the press force and retry using the heat method (oil bath at 80–100°C). The inner ring must never be driven in by hammer blows; impact can damage the cage or balls.
