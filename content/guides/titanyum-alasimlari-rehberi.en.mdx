<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Usage Note">
This guide details the structural and chemical selection criteria for titanium alloys (notably Ti-6Al-4V Grade 5 and Commercially Pure Grade 2) in aerospace, defense, marine, and medical implant applications in compliance with ASTM B348, AMS 4911, and ISO 5832-3. Account for titanium's low elastic modulus ($E \approx 110\text{ GPa}$) when sizing shafts and assessing springback.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

Titanium alloys offer exceptional specific strength (strength-to-weight ratio) and virtually complete immunity to atmospheric and marine corrosion. However, integrating titanium without understanding its fundamental mechanical and thermal idiosyncrasies triggers severe engineering and cost penalties:
- Twice the elastic deflection and compliance compared to structural steel due to its low elastic modulus, leading to chatter and dimensional instability,
- Severe tool wear and thermal breakdown in CNC machining caused by exceptionally low thermal conductivity ($k \approx 7\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) concentrating heat at the tool edge,
- Severe galling and thread cold-welding when fasteners or sliding interfaces lack appropriate barrier coatings.

## Assumptions

- Grade 5 alloy (Ti-6Al-4V / UNS R56400) is evaluated in the mill-annealed state.
- Continuous operating temperatures remain below $350 - 400\text{ °C}$ to prevent interstitial embrittlement from oxygen and hydrogen uptake.
- Compatibility with carbon fiber reinforced polymers (CFRP) is considered for galvanic corrosion isolation.

## Step by step method

1. **Select the Applicable Titanium Alloy Grade:**
   - **Grades 1 to 4 (Commercially Pure - CP Titanium):** Yield strength $170 - 480\text{ MPa}$; unmatched resistance to wet chlorine, organic acids, and marine immersion; easily formable and weldable.
   - **Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V - $\alpha+\beta$ Alloy):** Yield strength $R_{p0.2} \ge 830\text{ MPa}$, ultimate strength $950\text{ MPa}$; workhorse for aerospace airframes, aero-engine discs, and high-performance motorsports.
   - **Grade 23 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI):** Extra-low interstitial variant optimized for cryogenic toughness and surgical orthopaedic implants.

2. **Evaluate Specific Strength (Strength-to-Density Ratio):**
   Compare structural efficiency across material candidates ($\sigma / \rho$):
   - AISI 4340 Alloy Steel: $\frac{1000\text{ MPa}}{7.85\text{ g/cm}^3} \approx 127\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m/kg}$
   - Aluminum 7075-T6: $\frac{540\text{ MPa}}{2.81\text{ g/cm}^3} \approx 192\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m/kg}$
   - Ti-6Al-4V Grade 5: $\frac{950\text{ MPa}}{4.43\text{ g/cm}^3} \approx \mathbf{214\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m/kg}}$
   Titanium delivers up to a **40% to 55% direct weight reduction** over high-strength steel.

3. **Compensate for Low Elastic Modulus Deflection:**
   Because $E_{Ti} \approx 110\text{ GPa}$ is roughly half that of structural steel ($210\text{ GPa}$), identical cross-sections deflect roughly twice as much under bending:
   $$f_{Ti} = \frac{E_{steel}}{E_{Ti}} \cdot f_{steel} \approx 1.91 \cdot f_{steel}$$
   Rigidity-driven shafts and spindles require increased section moments of inertia ($I$).

4. **Establish CNC Milling and Turning Parameters:**
   - Low surface speed: $v_c = 40 - 70\text{ m/min}$ using rigid sub-micron carbide tooling with TiAlN or AlTiN PVD coatings.
   - High chip load: $f_z = 0.08 - 0.15\text{ mm/tooth}$ to cut beneath the work-hardened sub-surface zone.
   - High-pressure coolant: Direct minimum 20 to 70 bar flood delivery into the shear zone.

5. **Industrial Worked Example:**
   An aerospace structural tie-rod transmits a tensile design load of 45 kN with a design factor of safety $S_f = 1.5$:
   - Required tensile area:
     $$A_{req} = \frac{45\,000 \times 1.5}{830\text{ MPa}} = \mathbf{81.3\text{ mm}^2}$$
   - Weight for a 200 mm length tie-rod:
     $$m_{Ti} = (81.3 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2) \times 0.20\text{ m} \times 4430\text{ kg/m}^3 = \mathbf{0.072\text{ kg}}\ (72\text{ g})$$
   - Comparison with quenched & tempered 42CrMo4 steel ($S_y = 600\text{ MPa}$, $\rho = 7850\text{ kg/m}^3$):
     $$A_{req,steel} = \frac{67\,500}{600} = 112.5\text{ mm}^2 \implies m_{steel} = 112.5 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.20 \times 7850 = \mathbf{0.177\text{ kg}}\ (177\text{ g})$$
   - *Net Savings:* 105 grams (59.3% mass reduction) per structural tie-rod.

## Common mistakes

- **Running Tooling at Steel Surface Speeds:** Exceeding $v_c = 90\text{ m/min}$ creates catastrophic thermal tool failure within seconds due to titanium's low thermal dissipation.
- **Ignoring Springback in Sheet Metal Forming:** Titanium springs back twice as much as cold-rolled steel upon die release; hot forming or creep forming at $550 - 650\text{ °C}$ is standard practice.
- **Dry Assembly of Fasteners (Galling):** Uncoated titanium threads seize instantly during torque tightening; solid film MoS2 or silver plating must be specified.
- **Direct Aluminum-CFRP Contact:** Aluminum forms an aggressive galvanic couple with carbon fiber in moist marine environments, whereas titanium is galvanically compatible with CFRP.
