<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Usage Note">
This guide details the structural and mechanical sizing of cylindrical interference press-fit and shrink-fit connections according to ISO 286-1 and DIN 7190-1. For thin-walled hubs, von Mises equivalent stresses must be kept below the material yield strength to avoid bursting.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

Interference fits provide keyless, backlash-free, and high-fatigue-strength mechanical joints capable of transmitting severe alternating torque and axial forces. Flawed tolerance pairing induces two catastrophic failure modes:
- **Insufficient Interference ($\Delta_{min}$):** Joint slippage under operational loads, severe fretting corrosion, and rapid fretting fatigue failure.
- **Excessive Interference ($\Delta_{max}$):** Hub tensile stress exceeding material yield, causing radial fracture, severe galling during mechanical pressing, or bore plastification.

## Assumptions

- Both shaft and hub materials behave elastically and follow Hooke's law without bulk yielding.
- Contact interface pressure ($p$) is uniformly distributed over the engagement length $L$.
- Surface roughness asperities flatten plastically during assembly, diminishing the effective operational interference ($\Delta_{eff} < \Delta_{nominal}$).
- For thermal shrink fitting, heating is uniform and does not exceed the material tempering threshold ($T < 250\text{ °C}$).

## Step by step method

1. **Select ISO 286-1 Standard Fit Pairing:**
   - **H7/r6:** Medium drive fit; used for demountable gear rings, couplings, and bearing inner rings.
   - **H7/s6:** Permanent heavy-duty interference fit; standard for rigid shaft-hub torque transmission.
   - **H7/u6:** Heavy press fit; for massive torque, severe shock reversals, and thick-walled assemblies.

2. **Calculate Effective Interference ($\Delta_{eff}$) Corrected for Asperity Flattening:**
   Surface roughness peaks ($R_z$ [$\mu\text{m}$]) flatten during press assembly (DIN 7190):
   $$\Delta_{eff} = \Delta_{nominal} - 1.2 \cdot (R_{z,shaft} + R_{z,hub}) \quad [\mu\text{m}]$$

3. **Determine Interface Contact Pressure ($p$) via Lamé Equations:**
   For a solid steel shaft ($d_i = 0$, $d$) and outer steel hub ($d, D_o$) with identical elastic modulus ($E = 210\,000\text{ MPa}$, $\nu = 0.3$):
   $$p = \frac{\Delta_{eff}}{d} \cdot \frac{E}{2} \cdot \left( 1 - \frac{d^2}{D_o^2} \right) \quad [\text{MPa}]$$

4. **Verify Transmissible Torque ($T$) and Axial Retention Force ($F_{ax}$):**
   With engagement length $L$, diameter $d$, and friction coefficient ($\mu \approx 0.12 - 0.15$ dry clean press):
   $$T = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \pi \cdot d^2 \cdot L \cdot p_{min} \cdot \mu \quad [\text{N}\cdot\text{m}]$$
   $$F_{ax} = \pi \cdot d \cdot L \cdot p_{min} \cdot \mu \quad [\text{N}]$$

5. **Verify Hub Maximum von Mises Stress:**
   Peak tangential tensile stress develops at the inner bore diameter of the hub:
   $$\sigma_{t,max} = p_{max} \cdot \frac{D_o^2 + d^2}{D_o^2 - d^2}$$
   von Mises equivalent stress check:
   $$\sigma_{vm} = \sqrt{\sigma_{t,max}^2 + \sigma_{t,max} \cdot p_{max} + p_{max}^2} \le \frac{S_y}{S_f}$$

6. **Compute Thermal Shrink-Fit Heating Requirements:**
   To assemble without hydraulic press force, the hub expansion must clear the maximum interference plus assembly clearance ($\delta_a \approx 0.001 \cdot d$):
   $$\Delta T = \frac{\Delta_{max} + \delta_a}{\alpha \cdot d}$$

7. **Industrial Worked Example:**
   A solid steel shaft of $d = 50\text{ mm}$ is fitted to a C45 hub ($S_y = 360\text{ MPa}$) with outer diameter $D_o = 100\text{ mm}$ and length $L = 60\text{ mm}$ using an H7/s6 tolerance pairing:
   - Hole H7 ($0 / +25\ \mu\text{m}$), Shaft s6 ($+43 / +59\ \mu\text{m}$).
   - $\Delta_{min} = 43 - 25 = 18\ \mu\text{m}$, $\Delta_{max} = 59 - 0 = 59\ \mu\text{m}$.
   - Asperity loss for ground surfaces: $1.2 \times (3 + 3) \approx 7.2\ \mu\text{m}$.
   - Effective minimum interference: $\Delta_{eff,min} = 18 - 7.2 = 10.8\ \mu\text{m} = 0.0108\text{ mm}$.
   - Minimum interface pressure:
     $$p_{min} = \frac{0.0108}{50} \cdot \frac{210\,000}{2} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{50^2}{100^2}\right) = 0.000216 \times 105\,000 \times 0.75 = \mathbf{17.0\text{ MPa}}$$
   - Transmissible torque with $\mu = 0.14$:
     $$T = 0.5 \times \pi \times 0.050^2 \times 0.060 \times (17.0 \times 10^6) \times 0.14 = \mathbf{560.7\text{ Nm}}$$
   - Under $\Delta_{max}$, hub maximum hoop stress $\sigma_t \approx 145\text{ MPa} < 360 / 1.5 = 240\text{ MPa}$ (Fully safe against bursting).
   - Thermal expansion required for zero-force sliding assembly ($\alpha = 12 \times 10^{-6}\text{ 1/°C}$):
     $$\Delta T = \frac{0.059 + 0.030}{12 \times 10^{-6} \times 50} \approx \mathbf{148\text{ °C}} \implies \text{Heat hub to 170 °C.}$$

## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring Asperity Flattening:** On coarse-turned components, surface peaks flatten during pressing, destroying up to 40% of the calculated interference and resulting in joint slippage.
- **Using Uncontrolled Lubricants in Dry Friction Fits:** Applying heavy anti-seize paste drops friction coefficient $\mu$ from $0.15$ to $0.06$, slashing torque capacity by 60%.
- **Overheating Hubs Above 250 °C:** Heating quenched and tempered alloy hubs above 250 °C triggers temper embrittlement and permanent hardness degradation.
- **Omitting Lead-in Chamfers:** Forcing square-shouldered shafts into bores causes metal shaving and galling; a minimum $15^\circ - 20^\circ$ lead-in chamfer is mandatory.
