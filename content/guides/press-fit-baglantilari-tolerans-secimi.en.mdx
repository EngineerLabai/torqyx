<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Usage Note">
This guide details the structural and mechanical sizing of cylindrical interference press-fit and shrink-fit connections according to ISO 286-1 and DIN 7190-1. For thin-walled hubs, von Mises equivalent stresses must be kept below the material yield strength to avoid bursting.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

Interference fits provide keyless, backlash-free, and high-fatigue-strength mechanical joints capable of transmitting severe alternating torque and axial forces. Flawed tolerance pairing induces two catastrophic failure modes:
- **Insufficient Interference (`Δ_min`):** Joint slippage under operational loads, severe fretting corrosion, and rapid fretting fatigue failure.
- **Excessive Interference (`Δ_max`):** Hub tensile stress exceeding material yield, causing radial fracture, severe galling during mechanical pressing, or bore plastification.

## Assumptions

- Both shaft and hub materials behave elastically and follow Hooke's law without bulk yielding.
- Contact interface pressure (`p`) is uniformly distributed over the engagement length `L`.
- Surface roughness asperities flatten plastically during assembly, diminishing the effective operational interference (`Δ_eff &lt; Δ_nominal`).
- For thermal shrink fitting, heating is uniform and does not exceed the material tempering threshold (`T &lt; 250  °C`).

## Step by step method

1. **Select ISO 286-1 Standard Fit Pairing:**
   - **H7/r6:** Medium drive fit; used for demountable gear rings, couplings, and bearing inner rings.
   - **H7/s6:** Permanent heavy-duty interference fit; standard for rigid shaft-hub torque transmission.
   - **H7/u6:** Heavy press fit; for massive torque, severe shock reversals, and thick-walled assemblies.

2. **Calculate Effective Interference (`Δ_eff`) Corrected for Asperity Flattening:**
   Surface roughness peaks (`R_z` [`μ m`]) flatten during press assembly (DIN 7190):
   
`Δ_eff = Δ_nominal - 1.2  *  (R_z,shaft + R_z,hub)   [μ m ]`


3. **Determine Interface Contact Pressure (`p`) via Lamé Equations:**
   For a solid steel shaft (`d_i = 0`, `d`) and outer steel hub (`d, D_o`) with identical elastic modulus (`E = 210,000  MPa`, `ν = 0.3`):
   
`p = fracΔ_effd  *  (E / 2)  *  ( 1 - (d^2 / D_o^2) )   [ MPa ]`


4. **Verify Transmissible Torque (`T`) and Axial Retention Force (`F_ax`):**
   With engagement length `L`, diameter `d`, and friction coefficient (`μ  ≈  0.12 - 0.15` dry clean press):
   
`T = (1 / 2)  *  pi  *  d^2  *  L  *  p_min  *  μ   [ N  *  m ]`

   
`F_ax = pi  *  d  *  L  *  p_min  *  μ   [ N ]`


5. **Verify Hub Maximum von Mises Stress:**
   Peak tangential tensile stress develops at the inner bore diameter of the hub:
   
`σ_t,max = p_max  *  (D_o^2 + d^2 / D_o^2 - d^2)`

   von Mises equivalent stress check:
   
`σ_vm = sqrtσ_t,max^2 + σ_t,max  *  p_max + p_max^2  <=  (S_y / S_f)`


6. **Compute Thermal Shrink-Fit Heating Requirements:**
   To assemble without hydraulic press force, the hub expansion must clear the maximum interference plus assembly clearance (`delta_a  ≈  0.001  *  d`):
   
`Δ T = fracΔ_max + delta_aα  *  d`


7. **Industrial Worked Example:**
   A solid steel shaft of `d = 50  mm` is fitted to a C45 hub (`S_y = 360  MPa`) with outer diameter `D_o = 100  mm` and length `L = 60  mm` using an H7/s6 tolerance pairing:
   - Hole H7 (`0 / +25 μ m`), Shaft s6 (`+43 / +59 μ m`).
   - `Δ_min = 43 - 25 = 18 μ m`, `Δ_max = 59 - 0 = 59 μ m`.
   - Asperity loss for ground surfaces: `1.2  *  (3 + 3)  ≈  7.2 μ m`.
   - Effective minimum interference: `Δ_eff,min = 18 - 7.2 = 10.8 μ m  = 0.0108  mm`.
   - Minimum interface pressure:
     
`p_min = (0.0108 / 50)  *  (210,000 / 2)  *  (1 - (50^2 / 100^2)) = 0.000216  *  105,000  *  0.75 = mathbf17.0  MPa`

   - Transmissible torque with `μ = 0.14`:
     
`T = 0.5  *  pi  *  0.050^2  *  0.060  *  (17.0  *  10^6)  *  0.14 = mathbf560.7  Nm`

   - Under `Δ_max`, hub maximum hoop stress `σ_t  ≈  145  MPa  &lt; 360 / 1.5 = 240  MPa` (Fully safe against bursting).
   - Thermal expansion required for zero-force sliding assembly (`α = 12  *  10^-6  1/°C`):
     
`Δ T = frac0.059 + 0.03012  *  10^-6  *  50  ≈  mathbf148  °C  implies  Heat hub to 170 °C.`


## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring Asperity Flattening:** On coarse-turned components, surface peaks flatten during pressing, destroying up to 40% of the calculated interference and resulting in joint slippage.
- **Using Uncontrolled Lubricants in Dry Friction Fits:** Applying heavy anti-seize paste drops friction coefficient `μ` from `0.15` to `0.06`, slashing torque capacity by 60%.
- **Overheating Hubs Above 250 °C:** Heating quenched and tempered alloy hubs above 250 °C triggers temper embrittlement and permanent hardness degradation.
- **Omitting Lead-in Chamfers:** Forcing square-shouldered shafts into bores causes metal shaving and galling; a minimum `15^circ - 20^circ` lead-in chamfer is mandatory.

## ISO 286 Standard Tolerance Pairs Summary

ISO 286 defines the fundamental deviation (position of tolerance zone) and IT grade (width of tolerance zone). For interference fits, the following pairs are most commonly specified:

| Fit Code | Interference Range (for 50mm shaft) | Application |
|---|---|---|
| H7/g6 | -25 to -9 µm (clearance) | Sliding fit, easy assembly |
| H7/h6 | 0 to -16 µm (transition) | Location fit, push by hand |
| H7/k6 | +2 to +18 µm (transition) | Light press, precision positioning |
| H7/m6 | +4 to +20 µm (transition-interference) | Medium press, key-hub assemblies |
| H7/n6 | +8 to +33 µm (light interference) | Pressed fits, gear hubs |
| H7/p6 | +17 to +42 µm (interference) | Permanent assembly, no key needed |
| H7/r6 | +25 to +50 µm (interference) | High torque transmission |
| H7/s6 | +34 to +59 µm (heavy interference) | Drive fits, flywheels |
| H7/u6 | +54 to +79 µm (forced fit) | Maximum interference, thermal assembly |

**Selection rule:** H7/k6 and H7/m6 can be assembled by press without heat. H7/r6 and heavier require either a hydraulic press (> 50 kN for typical shafts) or thermal differential assembly. Never cold-press H7/u6 — the interference pressure will yield the shaft or hub bore material.

## Press Force vs. Thermal Assembly

**Press force calculation (Lame's thick-wall cylinder theory):**
`F_press = mu * P_fit * pi * d * L`

Where:
- `mu` = friction coefficient (0.10–0.15 for dry steel; 0.08–0.12 with lubricant)
- `P_fit` = interface pressure from interference (MPa), calculated from Lame's equation
- `d` = nominal diameter (m)
- `L` = engagement length (m)

Interface pressure for equal-material pair (E_hub = E_shaft):
`P_fit = (delta / d) * E / (1 + (d/D)^2) / (1 - (d/D)^2)`
where `delta` = interference (mm), `D` = outer diameter of hub (mm).

**Thermal assembly (shrink fit):** Heat the hub to:
`Delta T = delta / (alpha * d) + 10 °C (assembly clearance margin)`

For steel: `alpha = 11.5e-6 /°C`. Example: 50 µm interference on a 60 mm shaft:
`Delta T = 0.050 / (11.5e-6 * 60) + 10 ≈ 72 + 10 = 82 °C above ambient.`

Use an oven (not open flame) to avoid localized overheating and distortion. Maximum recommended heating temperature for most steels: 200 °C to avoid tempering and property loss in heat-treated components.

## Disassembly Planning

Press-fit disassembly is often more destructive than assembly. Plan for it at the design stage:

**Hydraulic nut / oil injection groove:** Machine a circumferential groove at the interference interface and provide a radial port for high-pressure oil injection (typically 200–300 bar). The oil expands the hub slightly, reducing friction by 80–95 % and enabling extraction with minimal force. This method is standard in large rolling mill and turbine shaft assemblies. Add to design drawings — retrospective drilling is expensive.

**Jackbolt holes:** Threaded holes in the hub face that accept jackbolts bearing against the shaft shoulder. Provides controlled axial force for extraction. Use equally spaced holes (minimum 3) to avoid cocking the hub during extraction.

**Sacrificial design:** If the hub (e.g., a bronze bushing) is the lower-cost component, design for destruction. A cut with a thin-kerf saw along one generator line relieves interference and allows hub extraction without shaft damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the practical difference between H7/s6 and H7/r6 for a gear hub?**
H7/s6 provides approximately 15–20 µm more interference than H7/r6 on a 50 mm shaft, translating to ~15 % higher torque transmission capacity without a key. Use H7/r6 when disassembly is planned during maintenance. Use H7/s6 when the joint is intended to be permanent or when keyless torque transmission is required in reversing-load applications.

**What happens if interference is too high?**
Excessive interference yields the hub bore material plastically. The effective diameter increases permanently, and after disassembly, the fit becomes loose. This is irreversible. Always check that assembly stress (hoop stress in hub): `sigma_hoop = P_fit * (D^2 + d^2) / (D^2 - d^2)` remains below the material yield strength with at least a 1.5× safety factor.

**Why does surface roughness matter for interference fits?**
Surface asperities at the interface partially "flatten" during press-fit assembly, reducing effective contact — the real interference is lower than the nominal by approximately `Ra_shaft + Ra_hub`. For tight fits (H7/s6, H7/u6), this reduction can be 10–20 µm on rough-turned surfaces. Specify Ra ≤ 0.8 µm for interference fit surfaces; grind or fine-turn to achieve this.
