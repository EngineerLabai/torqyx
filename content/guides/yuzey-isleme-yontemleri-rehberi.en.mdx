<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Usage Note">
This guide details industrial surface engineering, plating thicknesses, corrosion protection ratings, and dimensional tolerances according to ISO 1461, ISO 7599, ISO 4527, and ISO 9227 salt spray tests. For high-tensile Grade 10.9 and 12.9 fasteners, hydrogen de-embrittlement baking according to ISO 4042 is strictly mandatory.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

Even the highest-grade engineering alloys degrade catastrophically if exposed to harsh service environments without appropriate surface protection. Misjudging surface treatment parameters introduces severe engineering liabilities:
- Coating build-up (`5 - 80 ╬╝ m`) violating precision fit tolerances, jamming bearing journals and seizing threaded fasteners,
- Hydrogen embrittlement cracking in high-strength fasteners induced by acid pickling and electroplating, causing delayed brittle fracture under tension,
- Accelerating localized galvanic corrosion when incompatible plating metals contact the underlying substrate.

## Assumptions

- Atmospheric corrosivity categories are defined according to ISO 12944 (C1 through C5-M).
- Coating thickness build-up is factored into pre-plate machining drawing tolerances.
- External sharp edges feature generous corner break radii (`R  >=  1.0  mm`) to ensure uniform barrier thickness.

## Step by step method

1. **Select Coating Process for Application Duty:**
   - **Hot-Dip Galvanizing (HDG - ISO 1461):** `45 - 85 ╬╝ m` thickness; sacrificial cathodic protection providing 30+ years outdoor service life for structural steel.
   - **Electro-Galvanized Zinc (ISO 2081):** `8 - 15 ╬╝ m` thickness with blue/yellow trivalent passivation; cost-effective corrosion protection for precision mechanical fasteners.
   - **Hard Anodizing / Type III (ISO 7599):** `25 - 50 ╬╝ m` ceramic alumina layer; imparts `60  HRC` equivalent surface hardness, dielectric isolation, and wear resistance to aluminum.
   - **Electroless Nickel (ENP - ISO 4527):** `10 - 25 ╬╝ m` uniform autocatalytic deposit; 100% uniform thickness in blind bores, `50 - 65  HRC` hardness, and superior chemical resistance.
   - **Hard Chrome Plating:** `20 - 100 ╬╝ m`; standard for hydraulic cylinder rods, low friction coefficient (`╬╝  Ôëê  0.12`), and `68 - 72  HRC` abrasion resistance.

2. **Calculate Dimensional Shift from Plating Layer Build-Up:**
   External diameters increase by double the coating thickness (`t`), while internal bore diameters decrease:
   
`D_plated = D_machined + 2  *  t,   d_bore,plated = d_bore,machined - 2  *  t`

   On standard `60^circ` metric screw threads, flank deposition shifts pitch diameter by four times coating thickness:
   
`╬ö d_pitch = 4  *  t`

   *(e.g., A `12 ╬╝ m` zinc layer expands screw pitch diameter by `48 ╬╝ m`, consuming the entire 6g tolerance allowance).*

3. **Mandate Hydrogen De-Embrittlement Baking for High-Strength Fasteners:**
   Per ISO 4042, steels with tensile strength `R_m  >=  1000  MPa` (especially 10.9 and 12.9 grade bolts) must be baked at `200 - 220  ┬░C` for 4 to 24 hours **within 4 hours** of electroplating to drive out trapped atomic hydrogen.

4. **Specify Neutral Salt Spray (ISO 9227 NSS) Exposure Benchmarks:**
   - Commercial zinc with blue trivalent passivate: 72 - 120 hours to white rust, 240 hours to red rust.
   - Zinc-Nickel alloy plating (12-15% Ni): 720 - 1000 hours red rust resistance.
   - Electroless Nickel (25 `╬╝ m` high-phosphorus): 500+ hours neutral salt spray resistance.

5. **Industrial Worked Example:**
   A precision journal shaft of `d = 25  mm` with h6 tolerance (`0 / -9 ╬╝ m  implies 24.991 - 25.000  mm`) requires `15 pm 3 ╬╝ m` electroless nickel plating:
   - Bilateral coating build-up: `2  *  (12dots 18 ╬╝ m ) = +24dots +36 ╬╝ m`.
   - If machined to nominal 25.000 mm before plating, final diameter becomes `25.024 - 25.036  mm`, completely failing the bearing fit!
   - **Correct Engineering Solution:** Pre-plate machining dimensions are derated:
     
`d_pre-plate = [24.991 - 0.036,  25.000 - 0.024] = mathbf24.955dots 24.976  mm`

   - Post-plating dimensions will land precisely within the intended h6 tolerance limits.

## Common mistakes

- **Failing to Undersize Thread Flanks Before Plating:** Applying `20 ╬╝ m` zinc to standard 6g bolts prevents nuts from threading on assembly lines.
- **Overlooking 50% Penetration in Hard Anodizing:** Type III anodizing grows 50% inward into the substrate and 50% outward beyond the original surface.
- **Omitting Vent and Drain Holes for Hot-Dip Galvanizing:** Dipping enclosed tubular weldments into `450  ┬░C` molten zinc without pressure relief holes causes explosive blowouts.
- **Omitting Hydrogen Bake Documentation on Critical Bolts:** Unbaked 10.9/12.9 bolts crack under preload within hours of assembly through silent delayed hydrogen fracture.

## Coating Thickness Measurement Methods

Measuring coating thickness after application is essential for quality control. Different coating types and substrate materials require different measurement techniques.

| Method | Principle | Suitable For | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magnetic Induction | Eddy current in ferromagnetic substrate | Non-magnetic coatings on steel (paint, zinc, chrome) | +/- 1-3 % |
| Eddy Current | Phase shift in non-ferromagnetic substrate | Anodizing, nickel on aluminum | +/- 2-5 % |
| X-Ray Fluorescence (XRF) | Element-specific X-ray emission | Multi-layer coatings; precious metal plating | +/- 1-2 % |
| Metallographic Cross-Section | Physical measurement of polished cross-section | Any coating; destructive; most accurate | +/- 0.5 µm |
| Ultrasonic | Sound wave reflection from interface | Thick coatings (paint, thermal spray) on any substrate | +/- 5 % |

**Calibration requirement:** Magnetic and eddy current gauges must be calibrated on certified reference standards matching the substrate material and surface finish. Surface roughness under Ra 3.2 µm is required for reliable readings. On rough blasted surfaces (Ra > 6 µm), use cross-section metallography for accurate measurement.

**Hard chrome plating verification:** Measure at minimum 5 points per 100 cm2 of plated surface per ISO 4042 sampling plan. Report minimum, maximum, and mean values. Thickness uniformity is a key quality indicator - excessive variation indicates poor current distribution in the plating bath.

## Environmental Regulations: RoHS and REACH Compliance

Surface treatment chemicals are heavily regulated. Two frameworks govern most industrial applications:

**RoHS (Restriction of Hazardous Substances, EU Directive 2011/65/EU):** Restricts 10 substances including lead (Pb), mercury (Hg), cadmium (Cd), and hexavalent chromium (Cr6+) in electrical and electronic equipment. Hard decorative chrome plating using Cr6+ (chromic acid bath) is restricted in RoHS-covered product categories.

**REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals, EU 1907/2006):** Hexavalent chromium compounds are on the SVHC (Substances of Very High Concern) list. Authorization is required for use in the EU after sunset dates. Trivalent chromium (Cr3+) plating is the primary alternative.

| Process | Cr6+ Status | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Decorative Chrome (Cr6+ bath) | Restricted (REACH SVHC) | Trivalent chrome (Cr3+), PVD chrome-look |
| Hard Chrome Plating | Restricted in EU (auth. required) | Electroless nickel, HVOF tungsten carbide |
| Cadmium Plating | Restricted (REACH) | Zinc-nickel alloy, aluminum-based |
| Yellow Chromate (Cr6+) | Restricted | Trivalent chromate (Cr3+) passivation |

**Transition note:** Trivalent chrome (Cr3+) baths now achieve corrosion resistance within 10-15 % of traditional Cr6+ at comparable film thickness. However, Cr3+ does not self-heal scratches like Cr6+ does - surface damage must be locally re-treated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the practical difference between electrolytic and electroless nickel plating?**
Electrolytic (electroplated) nickel uses an external current source. Deposit thickness varies with current density - edges and external corners receive more current and build up faster (high-point effect). Not suitable for complex geometries where uniform thickness is required. Electroless (autocatalytic) nickel uses a chemical reduction reaction - no external current. Deposit is perfectly uniform regardless of geometry, including blind holes and internal bores. This makes electroless nickel the preferred choice for precision bores, threaded features, and complex valve bodies.

**Is hard chrome plating the same as decorative chrome?**
No. Hard chrome (engineering chrome) is a thick (20-500 µm), dense chromium deposit applied for wear resistance and lubricity. Applied from Cr6+ bath; high hardness (950-1050 HV). Decorative chrome is a thin (0.2-0.5 µm) bright layer applied over a nickel undercoat for appearance only. The two are completely different processes with different bath chemistries, current densities, and application purposes. "Triple chrome" (copper + nickel + chrome) is the standard decorative automotive stack.

**Can materials other than aluminum be anodized?**
Anodizing (electrochemical oxide formation) works on any metal that forms a stable, adherent oxide in the correct electrolyte. In practice: aluminum is the primary industrial application (sulfuric acid bath, ISO 7599). Titanium anodizing produces colored oxide films (color controlled by voltage, not dye) used in medical implants and jewelry. Magnesium can be anodized (plasma electrolytic oxidation / PEO process) for corrosion and wear protection. Steel cannot be anodized in the traditional sense - phosphating is the functional equivalent for steel.
