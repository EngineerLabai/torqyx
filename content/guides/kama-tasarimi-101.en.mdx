<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Usage Note">
This guide details the strength verification of parallel shaft keys in accordance with DIN 6885-1 and ISO 773, as well as mechanical friction wedge locking mechanisms. For severe reversing torque or high-impact drives, interference press-fits, taper bushes, or involute splines are recommended.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

DIN 6885 parallel keys (feather keys) are the most prevalent mechanical coupling elements used to transmit torque between rotating shafts and gears, pulleys, or couplings. Likewise, taper wedges in tooling provide high clamping forces. Inadequate key sizing leads to:
- Excessive compressive surface pressure resulting in plastic crushing (bearing failure) and rotational backlash,
- Direct key shear failure along the shaft-hub interface under shock loads,
- Backwards slipping (loss of self-locking) in mechanical wedge clamps under dynamic vibration.

## Assumptions

- Key material is medium-carbon or alloy engineering steel (e.g., C45E or 16MnCr5) with yield strength equal to or greater than shaft and hub materials.
- Tangential force is uniformly distributed along the effective key engagement length (shaft torsional twist distortion is neglected).
- In round-ended keys (DIN 6885 Form A), the semicircular ends do not bear load; effective length is `l_k = l - b`.
- The friction coefficient (`μ`) between steel wedge surfaces is assumed constant for dry or lubricated contact.

## Step by step method

1. **Calculate Transmitted Tangential Force (`F_t`):**
   From shaft diameter `d` [`m`] and nominal operating torque `T` [`Nm`]:
   
`F_t = (2  *  T / d)`


2. **Select Standard Key Cross-Section (DIN 6885-1 / ISO 773):**
   Standard dimensions for key width (`b`), height (`h`), and shaft seat depth (`t_1`) are selected based on shaft diameter (`d`):
   - For `d = 38dots 44  mm`: `b = 12  mm`, `h = 8  mm`, `t_1 = 5.0  mm`, and hub depth `t_2 = 3.3  mm`.

3. **Verify Hub Surface Bearing/Crushing Stress (`σ_p`):**
   Because the hub is usually the softer material, check the compressive stress over the effective engagement length `l_k`:
   
`σ_p = (F_t / l_k  *  t_2)  <=  σ_p,allow`

   *(Permissible crushing limits: Grey Cast Iron GG-25: `60 - 80  MPa`; Cast Steel GS-45: `90 - 120  MPa`; Quenched & Tempered C45: `120 - 150  MPa`)*

4. **Verify Key Shear Stress (`τ_k`):**
   Check shear across the transverse cross-section:
   
`τ_k = (F_t / b  *  l_k)  <=  τ_allow  ≈  0.6  *  R_e / S_f`


5. **Verify Wedge Clamping Angle and Self-Locking Condition:**
   In inclined mechanical clamping wedges, self-locking occurs when the wedge taper angle `α` satisfies:
   
`tan(α)  <=  μ implies α  <=  arctan(μ)`

   *(Lubricated steel: `μ  ≈  0.10 implies α  <=  5.7^circ`; Dry clean steel: `μ  ≈  0.15 implies α  <=  8.5^circ`)*

6. **Industrial Worked Example:**
   A drive shaft with `d = 40  mm` transmits `T = 300  Nm` torque to a cast steel GS-45 hub. A DIN 6885 Form A key is specified (`b = 12  mm`, `h = 8  mm`, `t_2 = 3.3  mm`, total length `l = 50  mm`):
   - Tangential force: `F_t = (2  *  300 / 0.040) = mathbf15,000  N`
   - Effective contact length: `l_k = 50 - 12 = mathbf38  mm`
   - Compressive bearing stress:
     
`σ_p = (15,000 / 38  *  3.3) = mathbf119.6  MPa   <=  σ_p,allow  ≈  120  MPa     (Safe)`

   - Key shear stress:
     
`τ_k = (15,000 / 12  *  38) = mathbf32.9  MPa  ll τ_allow  ≈  75  MPa     (High Safety Factor)`


## Common mistakes

- **Using Total Length for Rounded-End Form A Keys:** Semicircular ends do not carry tangential load; using `l` instead of `l_k = l - b` underestimates bearing stress by 20% to 30%.
- **Ignoring Application Peak Torque (`K_A`):** In reciprocating compressors or press drives, dynamic shock multipliers range from 1.5 to 2.5; calculations must evaluate peak torque.
- **Overlooking Keyway Stress Concentration:** Machining a keyseat into a rotating shaft introduces a severe stress concentration factor (`K_t  ≈  2.5 - 3.0`) in fatigue bending/torsion.
- **Insufficient Hub Wall Thickness:** The radial wall thickness of the hub above the keyway must be at least `1.5  *  h` to avoid fatigue bursting.
