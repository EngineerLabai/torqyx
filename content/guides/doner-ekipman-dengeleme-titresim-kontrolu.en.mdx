<Callout type="info" title="Engineering Usage Note">
This guide provides practical methodologies to verify rotor unbalance and evaluate vibration severity in electric motors, pumps, fans, and turbomachinery in compliance with ISO 21940-11 and ISO 20816-1. For high-speed critical machinery, modal analysis and dual-plane dynamic balancing are mandatory.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

In rotating machinery, any offset between the center of mass and the geometric axis of rotation generates centrifugal forces proportional to the square of rotational speed ($F_c \propto \omega^2$). Left unmitigated, unbalance forces cause:
- Rapid depletion of bearing L10 life and premature fatigue spalling,
- Bolt loosening, keyway fretting, and structural fatigue cracking at foundation supports,
- Destructive resonance and catastrophic shaft deflection when forcing frequencies align with system natural frequencies.

## Assumptions

- The rotor behaves as a rigid body operating safely below its first critical bending speed ($n_{operating} < 0.70 \cdot n_{critical}$).
- Bearing housings and support structures provide symmetric, linear stiffness characteristics.
- Vibration velocity is measured as RMS ($v_{rms}$) across the standard 10 Hz to 1000 Hz frequency range according to ISO 20816-1.

## Step by step method

1. **Select Balancing Mode Based on Rotor Geometry:**
   - **Single-Plane (Static Balancing):** For disc-shaped rotors where length-to-diameter ratio is small ($L/D < 0.20$ and $n < 1000\text{ rpm}$), such as narrow pulleys, flywheels, and grinding wheels.
   - **Dual-Plane (Dynamic Balancing):** For elongated cylindrical rotors ($L/D \ge 0.20$ or higher operating speeds), such as electric motor armatures, multi-stage pump impellers, and industrial fans.

2. **Determine the ISO 21940-11 Balance Quality Grade ($G$):**
   - **G6.3:** Standard industrial fans, gearbox shafts, agricultural equipment.
   - **G2.5:** Electric motors, machine tool spindles, gas/steam turbines, process compressors.
   - **G1.0:** High-precision grinding spindles, gyroscopes, turbocharger components.

3. **Calculate Permissible Residual Unbalance ($U_{per}$):**
   With total rotor mass $m$ [$\text{kg}$] and angular velocity $\omega = \frac{2\pi \cdot n}{60}$ [$\text{rad/s}$]:
   $$U_{per} = 1000 \cdot \frac{G \cdot m}{\omega} \quad [\text{g}\cdot\text{mm}]$$
   For dual-plane balancing, distribute $U_{per}$ between the two correction planes based on distance from the center of gravity ($U_1 = U_2 = U_{per} / 2$ for symmetric rotors).

4. **Quantify Unbalance Dynamic Centrifugal Force:**
   The cyclic force transmitted into radial bearings is:
   $$F_c = m_u \cdot r \cdot \omega^2 = \frac{U_{per}}{1000} \cdot \omega^2 \quad [\text{N}]$$

5. **Evaluate ISO 20816 Vibration Severity Zones:**
   Evaluate overall bearing housing RMS velocity ($v_{rms}$ [$\text{mm/s}$]):
   - **Zone A (New Machine / Commissioning):** $v_{rms} < 1.4\text{ mm/s}$ (Medium industrial motors)
   - **Zone B (Unrestricted Long-Term Operation):** $1.4 \le v_{rms} \le 2.8\text{ mm/s}$
   - **Zone C (Restricted Operation / Plan Maintenance):** $2.8 < v_{rms} \le 4.5\text{ mm/s}$
   - **Zone D (Unacceptable / Immediate Shutdown):** $v_{rms} > 4.5\text{ mm/s}$

6. **Industrial Worked Example:**
   An industrial electric motor rotor with mass $m = 60\text{ kg}$ operates at $n = 3000\text{ rpm}$. The target balancing grade is G2.5:
   - Angular velocity: $\omega = \frac{2\pi \times 3000}{60} = \mathbf{314.16\text{ rad/s}}$
   - Maximum permissible residual unbalance:
     $$U_{per} = 1000 \cdot \frac{2.5 \times 60}{314.16} = \mathbf{477.5\text{ g}\cdot\text{mm}}$$
   - Shared between two symmetrical balancing correction planes:
     $$U_1 = U_2 = \frac{477.5}{2} = \mathbf{238.8\text{ g}\cdot\text{mm}}$$
   - At a bolt correction radius of $r = 120\text{ mm}$, the permissible unbalance mass tolerance is:
     $$m_{corr} = \frac{238.8}{120} = \mathbf{1.99\text{ grams}}$$

## Common mistakes

- **Attempting Single-Plane Balancing on Long Rotors:** Ignoring couple unbalance across the shaft length leads to high vibration once operating speed is reached.
- **Confusing Foundation Soft-Foot or Misalignment with Rotor Unbalance:** Misalignment produces dominant 2X shaft rotational frequency peaks, whereas unbalance is strictly 1X; adding trial weights to misaligned machinery exacerbates bearing damage.
- **Dwell Time During Resonance Pass-Through:** Passing through shaft critical speed slowly creates huge vibration amplitudes; speed ramp-up through critical zones must be accelerated via VFDs.
- **Ignoring Thermal Bowing at Operational Temperature:** Turbines and hot gas fans balanced cold can bow when heated, creating substantial thermal unbalance during steady-state service.
