This guide details parameter optimization (cutting speed, feed, depth of cut), cutting tool wear mechanics, and Taylor tool life equations across CNC lathe turning, milling, and precision grinding in accordance with ISO 3685 and DIN 6580.
</Callout>

## Problem / Objective

Across modern discrete manufacturing, machine hourly depreciation and cutting tool replacement costs constitute over 40 percent of total piece-part production expenditure. Machine shops routinely oscillate between two counterproductive operational extremes:
1. **Hyper-Cautious Machining:** Operators slash spindle speeds fearing tool breakage. This forces cutting edges to rub and burnish the workpiece rather than shear chips, triggering work-hardening and wasted spindle capacity.
2. **Thermal Overdrive:** Pushing surface speeds beyond the thermal envelope of tool coatings. When cutting interface temperatures breach 900 to 1100 °C, cobalt binder matrices within cemented carbide inserts diffuse, edges collapse plastically, and tools crater within minutes.

Engineering optimization mathematically balances Material Removal Rate (MRR) against tool life (T) via the empirical Taylor equation, targeting minimum production cost per component.

## Assumptions

- Manufacturer-recommended starting parameters (V_c, f_z, a_p) are used as the initial baseline.
- Machine rigidity and workholding security are adequate; vibration damping capacity is not ignored.
- Coolant strategy (emulsion, MQL, or HPC) is selected to match the specific workpiece-tool combination.
- Taylor's tool life equation is valid under constant operating conditions (same feed and depth of cut).

## Step by step method

1. **Calculate Cutting Speed (V_c) and Spindle RPM (n):**
   `V_c = (pi * D * n) / 1000` (m/min)
   Required spindle program speed: `n = (1000 * V_c) / (pi * D)`

2. **Determine Table Feed Rate (V_f) and Feed Per Tooth (f_z):**
   `V_f = n * z * f_z` (mm/min)
   Feed per tooth directly dictates undeformed chip thickness and edge loading.

3. **Calculate Material Removal Rate (MRR):**
   `MRR = (a_p * a_e * V_f) / 1000` (cm^3/min)

4. **Optimize Parameters Using Taylor's Tool Life Equation:**
   `V_c * T^m = C`
   For coated carbide, `m = 0.25 - 0.40`. Increasing cutting speed by 20 percent cuts tool life by approximately 60 percent! Maximum efficiency in roughing uses **moderate surface speed combined with high feeds and aggressive depth**.

5. **Real-World Case Study: Ti-6Al-4V Titanium Aerospace Milling:**
   - Standard steel parameters applied at `V_c = 140 m/min`. Titanium's low thermal conductivity trapped 75 percent of cutting heat at the tool tip; the tool chipped within 4 minutes.
   - Solution: `V_c = 55 m/min` + 5-flute variable-helix AlTiSiN coated end mill + 70-bar through-spindle HPC. Tool life extended from 4 to 85 minutes; piece machining cost dropped 65 percent.

## Common mistakes

- **Neglecting Toolholder Runout:** Holder TIR of 0.015 mm forces unequal chip loads across flutes, halving tool life. Hydraulic or heat-shrink toolholders are required for high-speed milling.
- **Conventional Instead of Climb Milling:** Modern CNC ballscrew machines must always climb mill; conventional milling rubs and burnishes at zero entry chip thickness, rapidly dulling cutting edges.
- **Excessive Grinding Depth of Cut:** Attempting 0.05 mm single-pass grinding to save cycle time opens invisible micro-cracks. Grinding passes must stay within 0.005 to 0.015 mm.
- **Ignoring Machine Spindle Power-Torque Curves:** Drilling large holes at low RPM below motor base speed starves the drive of torque, causing spindle stalls and tool fracture.
