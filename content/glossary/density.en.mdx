Density is mass per unit volume.

## Why it matters

It affects weight, inertia, buoyancy, and logistics cost.

## How it is used

In a design check, first define the relevant load, dimension, material, or flow condition. Then interpret this term by its role in the formula and compare it with limits.

## Design note

Density directly affects part mass, center of gravity, and inertia loads in moving systems. A lower density material can reduce total weight for the same geometry, but stiffness, manufacturability, cost, and availability must be checked at the same time. For fluids, density may change with temperature, so pressure-loss or buoyancy calculations should state the operating temperature.

<Formula label="Basic expression">
Density = Mass / Volume
</Formula>

<Callout type="info" title="Example">
For the same volume, a steel part can be about three times heavier than aluminum.
</Callout>
